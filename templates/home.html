How It Works

How Does Our Model Classify Garbage?

EcoSort AI uses a PreTrained model MOBILENETV2 trained on thousands of labeled waste images. The model learns visual patterns — texture, shape, color — unique to each category of waste.

Once trained, it can identify unseen waste images with high accuracy in under 2 seconds, even on a standard web browser.

  • Deep learning with pretrained model architecture
  • Transfer learning for higher accuracy
  • Image preprocessing and augmentation
  • Real-time inference on uploaded photos
  • 13-class multi-label prediction output
Recycling Impact

Recycling Techniques for Every Waste Type

Proper classification is only the first step. EcoSort AI also guides you on how to responsibly recycle or dispose of each type of waste — from composting organics to safely handling batteries.

  • Glass: Rinse and separate by color before recycling
  • Paper & Cardboard: Keep dry, flatten before recycling
  • Plastic: Check resin codes; avoid mixing types
  • Metal: Rinse cans; scrap metal is highly recyclable
  • Organic/Biological: Compost or biogas processing
  • Battery: Never bin — use designated drop-off points

Recycling Techniques

How to Recycle Each Waste Type

🔮

Glass Recycling

Separate by color (clear, brown, green). Rinse thoroughly. Never include ceramics, mirrors or light bulbs in glass recycling bins. Glass is 100% recyclable and can be reused infinitely without quality loss.

📦

Paper & Cardboard

Keep dry and clean. Flatten cardboard boxes to save space. Remove plastic tape, staples, and bubble wrap. Shredded paper may not be accepted at all centers — check locally.

🧴

Plastic

Check the resin identification code (number inside the triangle). Codes 1 (PET) and 2 (HDPE) are most widely recyclable. Rinse containers before placing in recycling. Avoid single-use plastics where possible.

🥫

Metal & Cans

Rinse aluminium and steel cans. Scrap metal should go to dedicated metal recyclers. Aluminium recycling uses 95% less energy than producing new metal from ore — making it one of the most valuable recyclables.

🌿

Organic & Biological Waste

Set up a compost bin for kitchen and garden waste. Many municipalities also offer food waste collection. Composted organic matter becomes rich fertilizer, returning nutrients to soil naturally.

🔋

Batteries & Hazardous

Never dispose of batteries in general waste. They contain lead, mercury, and cadmium. Use designated battery collection points at electronics stores or municipal centers. E-waste follows similar rules.

Model Architecture

Pretrained Models Used in EcoSort AI

We experimented with multiple deep learning architectures to achieve the best balance between speed and accuracy.

  • MobileNetV2 – Lightweight, fast, best performance (~89% accuracy)
  • MobileNetV3 – Improved efficiency with slightly better optimization
  • DenseNet – High accuracy but computationally heavy
  • EfficientNet – Very powerful but slower for real-time use
  • Custom CNN – Built from scratch but lower accuracy

Among all, MobileNetV2 gave the best trade-off between speed and accuracy, making it ideal for real-time web-based predictions.

Concept

CNN vs Pretrained Models

  • CNN (Custom Model) – Built from scratch using dataset
  • Requires large data and training time
  • Lower accuracy in our case
  • Pretrained Models – Already trained on huge datasets like ImageNet
  • Use Transfer Learning → faster training
  • Better feature extraction → higher accuracy

Pretrained models significantly improve performance because they already understand complex image features like edges, textures, and shapes.

Tools & Platform

Why We Used Google Colab

Our entire model training was done using Google Colab, a cloud-based platform that provides powerful GPU support for free.

  • Free access to GPU for deep learning training
  • No need for high-end local hardware
  • Easy integration with Python & TensorFlow
  • Supports large datasets and notebooks
  • Cloud-based → work from anywhere

GPU computation significantly reduces training time from hours to minutes by parallel processing thousands of operations simultaneously.
